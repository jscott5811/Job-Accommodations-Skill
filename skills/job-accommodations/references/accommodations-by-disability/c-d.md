# Accommodation Ideas by Disability (JAN)

Accommodation ideas from JAN's A-to-Z disability pages, extracted 2026-10-06. JAN materials are U.S. Government works. Verify against the live page before quoting — pages are updated periodically.

## Cancer
Source: https://askjan.org/disabilities/Cancer.cfm
- Decreased Stamina/Fatigue: anti-fatigue matting; ergonomic equipment; stand-lean stools; flexible schedule; job restructuring; periodic rest breaks; telework/work from home/working remotely; worksite redesign/modified workspace; walkers; wheelchairs
- Effect of/Receive Medical Treatment: flexible schedule; telework/work from home/working remotely
- Executive Functioning Deficits: calendars and planners; checklists; apps for memory/concentration; written instructions; recorded directives; timers; job coaches; on-site mentoring; extra time; speech recognition software
- Nausea: flexible schedule; mini refrigerators/electric coolers; odor control; telework/work from home/working remotely
- Respiratory Distress/Breathing Problem: air cleaners and purifiers; masks/respirators; odor control; flexible schedule; telework/work from home/working remotely
- Stress Intolerance: flexible schedule; job restructuring; modified break schedule; counseling/therapy; supervisory methods; support animal/person
- Suppressed Immune System: disability awareness/etiquette training; hand protection; physical/social distancing signage; policy modification; telework/work from home/working remotely; worksite redesign/modified workspace
- Temperature Sensitivity: flexible schedule; telework/work from home/working remotely; cooling clothing and fans; heated clothing and gloves; workstation space heaters
- Work-Related Functions (Air Quality/Irritants; Policies; Stress; Temperature): air cleaners/purifiers; masks/respirators; odor control; flexible schedule; modified break schedule; periodic rest breaks; policy modification; reassignment; telework/work from home/working remotely; task rotation
Example: A lawyer with cancer having concentration lapses from medication got uninterrupted work time and two work-from-home days per week. (Also: a meat packer with cancer-related fatigue got a flexible schedule with tasks shifted to specialty-order requests made weeks ahead.)

## Cataplexy
Source: https://askjan.org/disabilities/Cataplexy.cfm
- Balancing: canes, crutches, walkers, rollators, scooters; stair assists and stair lifts; grab bars; fall protection; personal safety and fall alert devices; padded edging
- Handling/Fingering: grip aids; ergonomic and pneumatic tools; alternative input devices; speech recognition software; one-handed keyboards; aide/assistant/attendant; periodic rest breaks
- Stress Intolerance: flexible schedule; job restructuring; modified break schedule; counseling/therapy; employee assistance program; supervisory methods; support animal/person
- Walking: scooters; walkers; wheelchairs
- Weak Speech: flexible schedule; job restructuring; voice amplification; outgoing voice amplification - telephone
- Work-Related Functions (Parking; Work Site Access; Work Station Access): accessible parking space; flexible schedule; telework/work from home/working remotely; wheelchairs; accessible workstations; automatic door openers; ramps; stair assists; worksite redesign/modified workspace
Example: A paralegal with hand weakness after cataplectic attacks got a one-handed keyboard and speech recognition software. (Also: an accountant whose attacks increased with stress got his position restructured to essential functions only plus an EAP referral; a secretary who fell out of her chair during attacks got an office chair with armrests and a lap belt.)

Example: An accountant with cerebral palsy who could not walk long distances was given a reserved parking space close to the building and allowed to work from home three days a week.
Example: An office assistant with cerebral palsy who had difficulty typing due to mild spasticity in her upper extremities was given a keyguard over her keyboard and forearm supports to stabilize her arms.
Example: A janitor with cerebral palsy and balance problems who had trouble carrying items and climbing ladders was given a motorized cart and a rolling safety ladder.

## Diabetes
Source: https://askjan.org/disabilities/Diabetes.cfm
- Hypo/Hyperglycemia: allow storage of medications (insulin) and/or food; provide an area to test blood sugar levels; provide an area to administer medications (insulin); provide appropriate containers for needle/syringe disposal; provide a rest area for reorientation after hypo/hyperglycemic episode; allow frequent breaks for food as needed; provide appropriate food for office-sponsored events; consider modifications to policies involving food storage and consumption; allow service animal access
- Neuropathy: modify job tasks requiring fine finger dexterity; provide protective clothing and equipment; eliminate or reduce the need to use sharp objects; provide anti-fatigue mats or padded carpeting; allow flexibility to sit or stand; provide parking accommodations
- Decreased Stamina/Fatigue: allow frequent rest breaks; reduce or eliminate strenuous activities; provide anti-fatigue mats or padded carpeting; provide a rest area with cot; allow flexibility to sit or stand; allow job sharing; shorten work day and extend work week; task rotation; ergonomic equipment; telework/work from home/working remotely
- Progressive Vision Loss: external magnification devices or computer screen magnification software; Braille/tactile graphics/screen reading software; high contrast settings and themes; digital voice recorder; qualified reader; flexible schedule to use public transportation; alternative lighting (lamps/fluorescent light filters) for photosensitivity; closed circuit TV (CCTV); talking blood glucose monitors; large button phones; service animal; telework
- Take Medication: flexible schedule; medication reminders; policy modification; rest area/private space; verbal cues; voice recorders
- Cognitive (Memory Loss/Confusion): written job instructions and prioritize assignments; calendars and planners/electronic organizers; reminders; additional training time/training refreshers; job coaches; written instructions
- Dietary Needs: flexible schedule; mini refrigerators/electric coolers; policy modification
- Other: easy access to restroom facilities; flexible schedule or time off for dialysis treatment; telework from the dialysis site; avoid temperature extremes; educate coworkers on emergency procedures and hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia symptoms; reduce stress; allow time off for counseling or therapy
Example: A cafeteria worker with diabetes who had difficulty standing got anti-fatigue mats, a sit/stand/lean stool, and flexible rest breaks. (Also: an assembly line worker with frequent urination and neuropathy got an anti-fatigue mat, sit/stand/lean stool, and an in-house paging system to notify the supervisor when a replacement was needed for a restroom break; a retail associate needing hemodialysis three times a week got a store transfer plus FMLA leave.)

Example: A production worker in recovery needing evening counseling was excused from overtime on counseling days. / A call center employee with a history of substance use disorder who struggled with face-to-face performance discussions had her supervisor switch to giving performance feedback in writing.
