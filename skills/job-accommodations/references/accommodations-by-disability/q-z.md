## Respiratory Impairments
Source: https://askjan.org/disabilities/Respiratory-Impairments.cfm
- Maintain a clean and healthy work environment
- Condition, heat, dehumidify, or add moisture to the air as appropriate
- Provide extra rest breaks to get fresh air or take medication
- Create a smoke- and fragrance-free work environment
- Allow alternative work arrangements when construction is taking place
- Use alternative pest management practices
- Allow alternative communication (telephone, e-mail, instant messaging, fax, memos)
- Step-free entrance with automatic doors (max 5-lb opening force)
- Accessible route of travel to and from work areas
- Scooter or motorized cart for long distances
- Move workstation closer to frequently used equipment, materials, and rooms
- Modify workstation for wheelchair, scooter, or oxygen therapy equipment
- Keep materials and equipment within reach range
- Restrooms easily accessed from the workstation
- Review emergency evacuation procedures
- Modify work location
- Modify the attendance policy
- Provide leave as an accommodation
- Modified or part-time work schedule
- Workspace with working windows
- Maintain the HVAC system; test indoor air quality
- Air purification systems (building-wide or personal workstation)
- Reduce workplace pollutants; adjust indoor temperature to individual needs
- Institute a fragrance-free workplace
- Pre-notification of construction and cleaning
- Non-toxic building materials, furnishings, supplies, and flooring
- Non-toxic solvents, primers, stains, and paints

## Sarcoidosis
Source: https://askjan.org/disabilities/Sarcoidosis.cfm (ideas from JAN article https://askjan.org/articles/Sarcoidosis.cfm and the Accommodation and Compliance Series publication)
- Adjustable workstation or ergonomic equipment to limit physical exertion
- Parking spot close to the workplace
- Air purifiers; functioning HVAC; discontinue fragrances or harsh chemicals
- Modify dress code policy or provide customized uniforms/PPE for skin rashes and sores
- Flexible schedule allowing frequent rest breaks or leave time for pain management
- Magnification or text-to-speech technology for blurred vision
- Periodic rest breaks to reduce eyestrain and fatigue
- Anti-glare filters for computer screens
- Alternative lighting for photosensitivity; fluorescent light tube covers or light filters; cubicle walls/shields to diffuse harsh lighting
- Scooter, walker, or other mobility aid to reduce walking (cardiovascular symptoms)
- Padded edging on desk corners or hard surfaces (fainting episodes)
- Telework, flexible scheduling, and leave time
- Decreased stamina/fatigue: aide/assistant/attendant, anti-fatigue matting, ergonomic assessments and equipment, flexible schedule, job restructuring, periodic rest breaks, stand-lean stools, task rotation, telework, wheelchairs/scooters, worksite redesign
- Respiratory distress: air cleaners/purifiers, alternative cleaning supplies, low/no-odor paints, masks/respirators, odor control, personal air cleaner, powered air purifying respirator (PAPR), telework, voice amplification
Example: An applicant with sarcoidosis and painful light sensitivity was allowed desk lamps and individually controlled lighting sources. / An employee with breathing limitations who had to park far away was given a reserved parking space closest to the building.

Example: A grocery store bagger with SAD had trouble with an early schedule from oversleeping plus fall/winter fatigue and depression; accommodated with an afternoon schedule and a move to the front of the store where windows let sunlight into her workspace.
Example: An elementary teacher with SAD had temporary extreme fatigue from a medication change plus winter onset; accommodated with temporary removal of two extra duty requirements (early and late bus duties).

## Skin Conditions
Source: https://askjan.org/disabilities/Skin-Conditions.cfm
- Provide alternative personal protective equipment (PPE) or uniform
- Have PPE or uniform custom-made
- Exempt from uniform requirement unless job-related and consistent with business necessity
- Avoid infectious agents and chemicals
- Limit invasive procedures (activities that could worsen the skin condition)
- Schedule that avoids direct high-sun exposure
- Protective clothing; ultraviolet light filters; alternate lighting; UV filters for computer screens
- Frequent breaks to rest, use medications, or prepare specialized food
- Flexible scheduling and flexible leave
- Work from home
- Sensitivity training for co-workers
- Reduce or eliminate workplace stress
- Computer input devices and work surfaces that can be washed and sterilized
- Regular cleaning and sanitizing of office equipment, furniture, workstation, restroom, and break room
- UV sanitizing wand (only if the individual is not UV-sensitive)
- Access to a refrigerator
- Modify work-site temperature and dress code
- Fan/air-conditioner or heater at the workstation
- Work from home during extremely hot or cold weather
- Maintain the ventilation system and redirect AC/heating vents
- Office with separate temperature control
- Reduce or eliminate physical exertion
- Allow telephone calls to a support person, or attendance at support group meetings during business hours
Example: A waitress with psoriasis was allowed a long-sleeved version of the required uniform instead of short sleeves. (JAN Situations and Solutions)
