# SSA Benefits and Wage Reporting Reference (2026 figures)

All figures below are for **2026** and were verified against official SSA, POMS, or SCDHHS pages on 2026-10-06. SSA adjusts these figures every year — if the calendar year has changed, re-check the cited pages before quoting numbers.

## SSI (Supplemental Security Income) — 2026 income limits

Source: SSA 2026 COLA Fact Sheet — https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html

| Item | 2026 figure |
|---|---|
| Federal Benefit Rate (FBR), individual | **$994/month** |
| FBR, eligible couple | **$1,491/month** |
| Resource limit | **$2,000 individual / $3,000 couple** |
| 2026 COLA | 2.8% |

### How SSI counts income

Source: https://ssa.gov/oact/cola/incomexcluded.html

- **General income exclusion:** first $20/month of most income does not count.
- **Earned income exclusion:** first $65/month of earnings does not count (plus any unused part of the $20), then **one-half of the rest** does not count.
- Other exclusions: impairment-related work expenses (IRWE), blind work expenses, PASS set-asides, first $30/quarter of infrequent earned income, first $60/quarter of infrequent unearned income.

Countable income = gross income minus exclusions. Monthly SSI payment = FBR minus countable income.

### The practical income limit for SSI workers

- Countable income must be below the FBR ($994 individual) to get any payment.
- **SGA is not the SSI test after eligibility.** It matters only at the initial-claim stage. Once SSI is established, continued eligibility depends on countable income and resources.
- Practical break-even (no state supplement, earnings only): about **$2,071/month gross earned income** before the SSI cash payment reaches zero.

### Student Earned Income Exclusion (2026)

- SSI recipients under age 22 who regularly attend school: **$2,410/month** excluded, up to **$9,730/year**.

## SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance) — 2026 figures

Source: SSA 2026 COLA Fact Sheet — https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html

| Item | 2026 figure |
|---|---|
| SGA, non-blind | **$1,690/month** |
| SGA, blind | **$2,830/month** |
| Trial Work Period (TWP) monthly earnings threshold | **$1,210/month** |

### Trial Work Period

Source: Red Book — https://www.ssa.gov/redbook/eng/ssdi-only-employment-supports.htm?tl=0%2C1

- 9 months (they do not have to be consecutive) within a rolling 60-month (5-year) window.
- Full SSDI benefits are paid during TWP months **no matter how high earnings are**, as long as work activity is reported and the disabling condition continues.
- Any month with gross earnings above $1,210 (or more than 80 hours of self-employment) counts as a TWP month.
- TWP does **not** apply to SSI.

### Extended Period of Eligibility (EPE)

- Begins the month after the TWP ends. The **first 36 months** are the re-entitlement period.
- During those 36 months: benefits are paid in any month earnings are **below** SGA and suspended in months above SGA. If earnings drop, benefits restart **without a new application**.
- First month above SGA in the EPE: disability "ceases," but benefits are paid for that month **plus a 2-month grace period**.
- After the 36 months: benefits continue if the person is eligible for payment in month 37, until a month of SGA-level work or medical recovery.
- **Expedited Reinstatement (EXR):** if benefits ended because of work and the person later must stop, reinstatement is available within **5 years** with no new application — up to 6 months of provisional benefits (same or related disability, not currently performing SGA).

### IRWE (Impairment-Related Work Expenses)

Source: Red Book — https://www.ssa.gov/redbook/eng/ssdi-only-employment-supports.htm?tl=0%2C1

- Deducted from **gross earnings when deciding if work is SGA** (SSDI). Also **excluded from earned income when figuring the SSI monthly payment**.
- Must: enable the person to work; be needed because of a physical or mental impairment; be paid by the person and not reimbursed (by Medicare, Medicaid, an insurer, or an Employment Network); cost a "reasonable" amount (standard community charge).
- Examples: vehicle modifications for commuting, paratransit or taxis for disability-related transportation, attendant care around work, service animal costs, wheelchairs, prosthetics, copays and deductibles for treating the disabling condition, **expendable medical supplies (such as incontinence products)**, disability-related assistive technology.
- No time limits; need not be monthly; a one-time expense can be deducted in one month or spread across working months.

## SSI work incentives

Source: Red Book — https://www.ssa.gov/redbook/eng/ssi-only-employment-supports.htm?tl=0

### 1619(a) — special SSI cash payments while working

- SSI cash payments can continue **even when earned income is at or above the SGA level**. This provision removes the need for a trial work period or extended period of eligibility under SSI.
- Qualifies when: eligible for an SSI payment for at least 1 month before working at SGA level; still disabled; meets all other eligibility rules including income and resource tests.
- Payment is figured the same way as before (FBR minus countable income). Automatic — no special application, but keep SSA updated on work activity.

### 1619(b) — Medicaid continues when SSI cash stops

Source: https://www.ssa.gov/disabilityresearch/wi/1619b.htm

- If earnings reduce the SSI cash payment to zero, **Medicaid can continue** as long as the person: was eligible for SSI cash for at least 1 month; still meets the disability requirement; still meets other non-disability SSI requirements; needs Medicaid to keep working; and has gross earnings insufficient to replace SSI + Medicaid + publicly funded attendant care.
- **South Carolina 2026 threshold (disabled SSI beneficiaries): $44,963/year.**
- A higher individualized threshold is possible with IRWE, blind work expenses, a PASS, publicly funded attendant care, or medical expenses above the state average.
- If earnings later drop, SSI cash can restart **without a new application**.

### PASS — Plan to Achieve Self-Support

Source: Red Book — https://www.ssa.gov/redbook/eng/ssdi-and-ssi-employments-supports.htm?tl=3

- Set aside income and/or resources for a specific work goal (education, vocational training, assistive technology, starting a business). Set-aside amounts are **not counted** toward SSI eligibility or payment amount; a PASS can establish or maintain SSI eligibility and increase the payment.
- Must be: individualized; in writing (form SSA-545-BK preferred); have a specific achievable work goal and timeframe; show what will be set aside and how it will be used; keep funds separate; **approved by SSA** (PASS Specialist); periodically reviewed.
- SGA does not limit PASS use. Other agencies (housing, SNAP) may also disregard PASS-excluded income.

### Section 301 — continued payment while in VR

- If SSA finds medical improvement (or an age-18 redetermination ends eligibility), benefits can **continue while participating in an appropriate VR or similar program** that began before disability ended.
- SSA must determine that continued participation will increase the likelihood of permanent removal from the benefit rolls.
- Continues until: program completed, participation stops, or SSA decides it will no longer help end benefits dependence.
- Appropriate programs include: an IEP (ages 18–21), a Ticket to Work Individual Work Plan, a VR agency IPE, American Job Center plans, and a PASS.

## Wage reporting requirements

Source: SSA blog, posted 2026-10-01 — https://www.ssa.gov/blog/en/posts/2026-10-01.html; Choose Work! wage reporting library — https://choosework.ssa.gov/library/wage-reporting/

### Deadlines

- Report changes and wages by the **10th day of the month after they occur**.
- SSA encourages reporting by the **6th day** of the month after payday to reduce overpayments.
- Late reporting can result in **penalties that reduce the SSI payment**.

### How to report wages (SSI)

- **Payroll Information Exchange (PIE):** employer sends wages directly. Authorize with form SSA-8240 (online, office, or phone). Keep reporting another way until PIE confirmation arrives.
- **my Social Security account:** ssa.gov/myaccount
- **SSA Mobile Wage Reporting app** (free, Apple App Store / Google Play)
- **Telephone Wage Reporting** (automated system)
- Call **1-800-772-1213** (TTY 1-800-325-0778), Mon–Fri 8am–7pm
- Visit the local Social Security office
- **Before using an automated option — or when changing employers — call SSA with the employer's information and Employer Identification Number (EIN).**
- Free email/text monthly reminders available (no app needed).

### What must be reported (SSI)

- Gross wages for **every month** worked — for multiple jobs, each one.
- Any change in income (wages, pensions, unemployment), resources, or employment status (start/stop/change jobs).
- Changes in address, living arrangements, citizenship/immigration status, marital status.
- Changes affecting a spouse (if married and living together) or a child's parents must also be reported.

### SSDI / Ticket to Work — different cadence

Source: Choose Work! and SSA FAQ — https://Www.Ssa.gov/faqs/en/questions/KA-01412.html

- SSDI reporting is **event-driven**, not monthly: report promptly when starting or stopping work, and report changes in duties, hours, or pay, plus any new work expenses paid because of the disability (IRWE).
- Via my Social Security, SSDI beneficiaries can report wages up to **2 years** back (max 104 pay periods / 5 employers per session). SSI wages can be reported for the month prior — within the first 6 days if possible, any time during the month.
- Representative payees, spouses, parents, and sponsors of SSI recipients may also report wages online, by phone, or by app.
- Other options: fax, mail, or bring pay stubs to the local office. Certified mail is optional and at the sender's cost — not required.
- SSA gives a receipt when work activity is reported. **Save it** along with pay stubs.

### Consequence of not reporting

- Overpayments. Overpayments generally must be repaid. Reporting on time is the main defense.

## Medicare and Medicaid for working individuals with disabilities

### Extended Medicare coverage (SSDI)

Source: Red Book — https://www.ssa.gov/redbook/eng/extended-medicare-coverage.htm

- **At least 93 consecutive months (7 years 9 months)** of Medicare after the 9-month Trial Work Period — includes Part A (premium-free) and Parts B and D if enrolled.
- The 93 months start the **month after the last TWP month**.
- Qualifies when: the person already has Medicare, cash benefits stopped because of work above SGA, and the disabling impairment continues.

### Buying Medicare after premium-free coverage ends

- Continued Medicare can be **bought** (premium Part A at the same cost uninsured retirees pay; Part B premium too — Part B can only be bought with Part A; Part D also available) as long as the disability continues.
- Check current premium amounts at medicare.gov — the Red Book's dollar examples are stale (2019 figures).

### QDWI — Qualified Disabled and Working Individuals

Source: SSA POMS HI 00815.023, updated 02/26/2026 — https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/POMS.NSF/lnx/0600815023

- States are **required to pay Part A premiums** for working people with disabilities who meet income and resource standards, apply through the state Medicaid agency, and are ineligible for Medicaid on any other basis.
- **2026 QDWI limits (all states except AK/HI):** income limit **$5,405/month individual**; resource limit **$4,000 individual / $6,000 couple**. (Figures incorporate the $20 exclusion plus earned-income disregards.)

### Medicaid Buy-In for working people with disabilities

- Federal authority: the **Ticket to Work and Work Incentives Improvement Act of 1999** (signed 12/17/1999) lets states raise Medicaid income and asset limits for working individuals with disabilities (ages 16–64) and let them **buy into Medicaid** through premiums or sliding-scale cost-sharing, including people above 250% of the federal poverty level.
- Source: GAO report GAO-03-587 — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GAO-03-587/html/GAOREPORTS-GAO-03-587.htm

### South Carolina — "Working Disabled" program

Source: SCDHHS — https://scdhhs.gov/members/program-eligibility-and-income-limits

- SC uses the **Balanced Budget Act of 1997, Section 4733** optional coverage group: working disabled individuals with family income below **250% of the federal poverty level** get the full range of Medicaid services. Coverage began 10/1/1998.
- Eligibility: under 65; totally and permanently disabled under the SSI definition; SC resident; US citizen or lawful permanent resident; has or applied for an SSN; assigns medical-support rights; **working (has earned income)**.
- Financial test (two steps): (1) family's total net income after deductions must be at or below **250% FPL** for the family size; (2) the individual's **unearned** income must be at or below **100% FPL** for an individual.
- Countable resources must be below the **$9,950** Aged/Blind/Disabled resource limit (individual).
- Dollar equivalent of 250% FPL: confirm against the live SCDHHS income-limits table — third-party sites cite figures but the official page states the test in FPL terms. Re-check before quoting a dollar amount.
